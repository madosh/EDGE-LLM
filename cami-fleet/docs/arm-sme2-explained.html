On-device AI · explained simply

How your phone's CPU just became an AI engine

Big AI models used to need GPUs or special chips. New tools from Arm and Google now make ordinary CPUs run them fast — small enough to fit on a phone, quick enough to feel instant.

Based on: Google Developers Blog · May 14, 2026

In one paragraph

An AI model is mostly a giant pile of multiplications. Arm built a new feature into modern CPUs called SME2 that does those multiplications way faster. Google's LiteRT software automatically uses it. Together, they can generate 11 seconds of music on a phone in under 7 seconds — no GPU, no internet.

1

The problem they were trying to solve

Modern AI models (image generators, music generators, chatbots) are huge. Until now, running them on a phone meant two bad choices:

→ Run on the CPU: works everywhere, but slow.
→ Run on a specialized chip (NPU / GPU): fast, but only a few phones have one, and every phone vendor's chip is different.

Analogy

Imagine you need to deliver pizzas across a city. The CPU is a regular bicycle — every house has one, but it's slow. A GPU/NPU is a sports car — fast, but only some people own one, and every brand needs its own special fuel.

2

The hardware trick — Arm SME2

Arm (the company that designs the chips in almost every phone) added a new feature to their newest CPUs called SME2 — short for Scalable Matrix Extension 2.

An AI model spends almost all its time doing one type of math: multiplying big grids of numbers (called matrices). SME2 is a built-in calculator inside the CPU that does exactly that — and only that — extremely fast.

Analogy

It's like adding a pizza-folding robot to the bicycle. The bike still goes everywhere, but now there's a special tool clipped on for the one job you do over and over. The result: up to 5× faster AI math on the same CPU.

3

The software bridge — KleidiAI inside LiteRT

Hardware is useless without software that knows how to use it. So:

→ Arm wrote tiny ultra-optimized routines called KleidiAI that speak directly to SME2.
→ Google plugged KleidiAI into the math engine inside LiteRT (their on-device AI runtime, formerly TensorFlow Lite).

So if you just run your model with LiteRT on a modern Arm phone, it automatically uses SME2. The developer doesn't write any special code. No assembly, no hardware tricks.

Analogy

You drop your pizza order into the app and it picks the bike with the robot attachment automatically — you don't even know it's there.

4

How a model gets ready: Convert → Optimize → Deploy

Google bundles three tools that take a model from a research notebook all the way to running on a phone:

Step 1

Convert →

LiteRT-Torch turns a PyTorch model (research format) into a .tflite file (phone format).

Step 2

Optimize →

Model Explorer shows which parts of the model can be shrunk safely. AI Edge Quantizer shrinks them.

Step 3

Deploy

LiteRT runs the small .tflite file on the device, automatically using SME2 if available.

What does "shrink" mean?

Models normally store each number using 32 bits (very precise, but big). Quantization rounds them down to 8 bits (less precise, but 4× smaller and much faster). Done carefully, you can't hear or see the difference — but the model now fits comfortably in phone memory.

5

The proof — generating music on a phone

To prove it works, Google + Arm took Stability AI's Stable Audio Open Small model — which generates 11-second stereo music clips from text prompts — and ran it entirely on a phone. The results, compared to the unoptimized original:

14 s → 6.6 s
Generation time on a SME2 Android phone
10 s → 4.3 s
Generation time on a MacBook M4
~4×
Smaller memory footprint
~0%
Loss in audio quality

In other words: the optimized version is about twice as fast, four times smaller, and still sounds the same. And it runs entirely on the CPU — no GPU, no NPU, no cloud.

6

Why this actually matters

  • Reach. Almost every phone in the world has an Arm CPU. AI features no longer require a flagship phone with a special chip.
  • Privacy. Your prompts and outputs never leave the device. No server, no logs.
  • Offline. Works on a plane, in the subway, in a remote village. No internet needed.
  • Cost. No cloud GPU bills. The user's own CPU does the work.
  • Speed. No network round-trip — responses can start in milliseconds.

Quick glossary

CPU
The general-purpose brain inside every phone and laptop. Good at everything, great at nothing in particular.
GPU / NPU
Specialized chips designed for graphics or AI math. Faster than a CPU at those jobs, but not present on every device.
Arm
The company that designs the chip blueprints used in almost every smartphone (Apple, Samsung, Qualcomm, MediaTek all license from Arm).
SME2
A new add-on inside Arm's latest CPUs that does matrix math very fast — exactly what AI models need.
KleidiAI
Tiny pieces of Arm-written code that know how to drive SME2.
LiteRT
Google's runtime for running AI models on a device (formerly TensorFlow Lite). Uses KleidiAI under the hood.
XNNPACK
The math engine inside LiteRT. It's the part that actually calls KleidiAI.
Quantization
The trick of storing each number with fewer bits (e.g. 8 instead of 32). Makes models smaller and faster with almost no quality loss.
.tflite
The file format LiteRT uses to load a model on a device. Like an MP3 for AI models.
